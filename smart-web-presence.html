01 — What this is

Three problems, and looking good is none of them.

Solution 02 of 03 · the Be Trusted stage

A website that is legible to machines as well as people, wired directly into your booking or inquiry flow so nothing lands in a dead inbox, and kept current instead of needing a developer ticket for every small change.

Those are three different problems, and a site can look completely fine while failing all three.

What it can connect to

As much or as little of this as you want. A site that is only a site is a perfectly good outcome, and it is what a lot of businesses need. The rest is there when it earns its place:

  • Booking and enquiry — your calendar, so an enquiry becomes an appointment instead of an email somebody has to answer.
  • Your CRM — so the enquiry is recorded against the person rather than lost in an inbox.
  • Email follow-up — sequences that go out after the enquiry, written for what the person actually asked about.
  • SMS — reminders and replies by text, which is where a confirmation is most likely to be read.
  • Social replies — comments and messages on your accounts answered in the same place as everything else, instead of in four apps.

Each of these is a decision on its own, taken when it is worth taking. None of them is a condition of the website.

The argument in full is what a website has to do now that it didn’t have to do in 2019, with the research behind it. The machine-readable half is covered separately in schema markup in plain English.

How we build

  • You own it. Static, dependency-free, hosted in your own account. No monthly platform rent, no builder subscription that holds your site hostage.
  • It paints instantly. The first frame a visitor sees is already the finished design — no flash, no shift, no swap. That is what reads as expensive.
  • Facts as text. Prices, hours, services and answers are written text, never baked into an image. A machine can only quote what it can read.
  • One action. One thing you want a visitor to do, named the same way in every single place it appears.

Common questions

Our site already looks good. Why would that matter?

Looking fine to a person, being read correctly by a machine, and turning a visitor into a booking are three separate problems. A site can be handsome and lose on all three.

Do you handle hosting and maintenance?

Yes — and that’s the difference from a typical one-time build. It’s meant to be kept current, not handed over and forgotten. The hosting account stays in your name.

What if we already have a designer?

Then this is complementary rather than a replacement. It isn’t primarily a design service; it’s a functional one. Scope depends on where the friction actually is.
